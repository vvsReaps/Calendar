Your discretion, priced

The exit is a choice too · 1 NQ

Every signal prints both a trailing box and a full HTF target, and the member chooses per trade: trail out, or hold to the far target. This block prices that choice the same way the ladder above prices the session read. Every figure here is computed on the same 1,032 base entries with the exit as the only variable. Trail-everything on a per-entry basis matches the shipped record; hold-to-target on the same entries books $129,118, far below its own full-run figure of $114,722, because early exits change which later entries the quota and cooldown admit — a fact about trade sequencing, shown rather than hidden.

Always picking the worse exit ends at $-151,651 — deeply underwater; exit choice is where this system lives and dies. The shipped trail beats the coin flip by $64,512 with no per-trade skill required. Every extra 10% of exits called correctly is worth about $69,056 on one contract. And even with perfect exits, 245 of the 1,032 entries still lose: the record’s edge is the size asymmetry between wins and losses, not the hit rate; anyone selling you a 90% win rate is selling you the wrong thing. This prices discretion; it is not a performance claim.

Trailing the exit is what makes a funded account survivable

The resting-stop trail is not a compromise here: it earns more than running to the far target, $208,142 against $64,722, with thirteen positive months out of thirteen (untrailed: a 32R drawdown). Exits fill at the trail level itself, like the resting stop order it is.

It carries the Lucid run outright: 1 evaluation, 0 blow-ups, 141 payouts, $179,097 net. The evaluation passes in three days and the account never looks back.

Do not strip the account back to the buffer

Lucid lets you withdraw everything above $52,100, which leaves you on a $2,000 leash the next morning — the $1,200 daily loss limit means two bad days in a row from the floor is a blown account. This run withdraws only down to $54,000, keeping $1,900 of extra cushion above the buffer (three DLL days of room), and returns $179,097; draining to the floor pays about $1,700 more on this year and never blows up either — the cushion is cheap insurance against the sequence this year didn’t have.

Funded accounts use INTRADAY drawdown

Drawdown and the daily loss limit are both charged at each trade’s intrabar worst (its maximum adverse excursion), not just its close. The DLL is a soft breach as Lucid defines it: the day ends, the account survives. The funded max loss trails intraday and locks at $50,100. Not modelled: the red-folder news rule — funded accounts must be flat from one minute before to one minute after high-impact USD news, a hard breach. The bot does not have a news filter yet; add one before running this on a funded account.

Before you read the numbers

What this is, and what it is not

Read this before you read anything above.
  • Hypothetical backtested results, not a live track record.
  • Everything above is gross. Charged at half a point of slippage per leg plus $4 round-turn commission ($24 a trade, $24,768 across 1,032 trades), the year finishes at +$183,374, a +366.7% return; costs take roughly a tenth of the result.
  • Fills are modelled at the close of the signal candle and at the exact stop or target price. Real fills gap, slip and queue.
  • Past performance does not predict future results. A single year is one market regime, and the v98.4 configuration was selected against this same year of data — the live record is the only out-of-sample test.
  • The first target banks 30% of position. On a single NQ contract that is not executable; the SAFE TP floor (stop clamps to SAFE TP once touched) stands in for it, or use micros / two contracts.
  • Futures trading carries substantial risk of loss and is not suitable for every investor. You can lose more than your initial deposit. Nothing here is financial advice, a solicitation, or a recommendation.
  • Results come from an independently rebuilt bar-close replay engine (the third build of this model outside TradingView), not TradingView’s strategy tester; it reproduces the reference entry (2026-08-06 09:36 → target). Absolute setup counts may differ from other runs.
