Trailing the exit is what makes a funded account survivable

Running every trade to the far target earns more on paper — $78,013 against $55,046 — but it produces 62 trades that lose over $1,000 and a worst day of $3,610. Against a $2,000 loss limit that is fatal, repeatedly.

Closing on the model's trailing box instead leaves 2 trades losing over $1,000 and a worst day of $1,626. Through Lucid that is the difference between 23 evaluations, 22 blow-ups and $29,043 — and 2 evaluations, 1 blow-up and $42,580. You give up a quarter of the paper return and keep almost half again as much cash.

Do not strip the account back to the buffer

Lucid lets you withdraw everything above $52,100, which leaves you on a $2,000 leash the next morning. That returns $35,554 and blows 2 funded accounts. Leaving $1,900 of extra cushion returns $42,580. The withdrawal schedule is worth more than the strategy.

Funded accounts use INTRADAY drawdown

Modelled at trade close. Charge it at its harshest — assume every trade probes its stop before resolving — and the year still returns $3,840, where the untrailed version went negative. That margin is the trail.

Five identical accounts diversify nothing. They take the same trades at the same moment, so all 5 blow-ups land on the same day and every drawdown is multiplied rather than smoothed. Fills at five contracts are also not fills at one. And Lucid caps the sim profit released when you are moved to live at $15,000 in total, not per account — so a large stranded balance across five accounts does not all come with you.
Modelled with no partial at the first target, since 30% of one contract is not executable. Evaluation priced at $165 (50K, end-of-day drawdown with the daily loss limit). Lucid's news rule is not modelled — LucidDaily requires you flat from one minute before to one minute after high-impact USD releases, and this model's window straddles the 10:00 ET slot, so real trading would skip setups this simulation takes. Before you read the numbers

What this is, and what it is not

Read this before you read anything above.
  • Hypothetical backtested results — not a live track record.
  • Everything above is gross. Charged at half a point of slippage per leg plus $4 round-turn commission, the year finishes at $50,440, a +100.9% return — costs take roughly a seventh of the result.
  • Fills are modelled at the close of the signal candle and at the exact stop or target price. Real fills gap, slip and queue.
  • Past performance does not predict future results. A single year is one market regime — the final four months of this sample were considerably better than the first eight, for every configuration tested.
  • The first target banks 30% of position. On a single NQ contract that is not executable; it needs micros (3 of 10 MNQ) or a two-contract minimum.
  • Futures trading carries substantial risk of loss and is not suitable for every investor. You can lose more than your initial deposit. Nothing here is financial advice, a solicitation, or a recommendation.
  • Results come from bar-close reproductions of the indicator, not TradingView's strategy tester. Absolute setup counts may differ from other runs.
