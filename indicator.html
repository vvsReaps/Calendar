Your discretion, priced

The exit is a choice too · 1 NQ

Every signal prints both a trailing box and a full HTF target, and the member chooses per trade: trail out, or hold to the far target. This block prices that choice the same way the ladder above prices the session read. Every figure here is computed on the same 255 base entries with the exit as the only variable. The shipped record books $131,060 rather than the $180,206 shown for trail-everything here because early exits change which later entries the quota and cooldown admit; the difference is a fact about trade sequencing, shown rather than hidden.

And even with perfect exits, 59 of the 255 entries still lose: the record’s edge is the size asymmetry between wins and losses, not the hit rate; anyone selling you a 90% win rate is selling you the wrong thing. This prices discretion; it is not a performance claim.

Trailing the exit is what makes a funded account survivable

The resting-stop trail is not a compromise here: it earns more than running to the far target, $81,060 against $62,890, and leaves one basically-flat losing month (worst −$163 against four red months and an $11,000+ drawdown untrailed). Exits fill at the trail level itself, like the resting stop order it is.

It carries the Lucid run outright: 1 evaluation, 0 blow-ups, 51 payouts, $63,805 net. The evaluation passes in the first week and the account never looks back.

Do not strip the account back to the buffer

Lucid lets you withdraw everything above $52,100, which leaves you on a $2,000 leash the next morning. This run withdraws only down to $54,000, keeping $1,900 of extra cushion above the buffer, and returns $63,805. The withdrawal schedule is worth more than the strategy.

Funded accounts use INTRADAY drawdown

Modelled at trade close, with the trail filled at its level like a resting stop. Funded accounts charge drawdown at its intrabar worst: a trade that probes deep before resolving can breach the limit even when it recovers. The worst recorded day is −$3,260; the simulation’s $1,200 daily-loss lockout stops trading well before that in live Lucid conditions.

Before you read the numbers

What this is, and what it is not

Read this before you read anything above.
  • Hypothetical backtested results, not a live track record.
  • Everything above is gross. Charged at half a point of slippage per leg plus $4 round-turn commission ($24 a trade, $6,768 across 282 trades), the year finishes at +$74,292, a +148.6% return; costs take roughly a tenth of the result.
  • Fills are modelled at the close of the signal candle and at the exact stop or target price. Real fills gap, slip and queue.
  • Past performance does not predict future results. A single year is one market regime: the final four months of this sample were considerably better than the first eight, for every configuration tested.
  • The first target banks 30% of position. On a single NQ contract that is not executable; it needs micros (3 of 10 MNQ) or a two-contract minimum.
  • Futures trading carries substantial risk of loss and is not suitable for every investor. You can lose more than your initial deposit. Nothing here is financial advice, a solicitation, or a recommendation.
  • Results come from an independently rebuilt bar-close replay engine (the third build of this model outside TradingView), not TradingView’s strategy tester; it reproduces the reference entry (2026-08-06 09:36 → target). Absolute setup counts may differ from other runs.
