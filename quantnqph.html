04 / Start here

First understand the mechanism.

Gamma exposure

How will hedging react?

When a dealer sells an option, they hedge its directional risk in the underlying. GEX adds up how much those hedges are expected to change as price moves. It is a regime read: will dealer rebalancing dampen movement or amplify it?

Delta exposure

What is the hedge now?

DEX estimates the directional state of the aggregate dealer hedge at this moment. GEX describes the change in hedging behavior; DEX describes the current hedge state. They are separate axes, not one bullish/bearish score.

Gamma flip

Where can behavior change?

The flip, or zero-gamma level, is the estimated spot price where aggregate GEX changes sign. It is a useful line in the sand, but it is a projection built while holding OI fixed—not an observed law of the market.

POSITIVE_GAMMA

Compress & revert

Dealer hedging tends to sell strength and buy weakness, suppressing volatility and encouraging rotation or pinning.

NEGATIVE_GAMMA

Expand & accelerate

Dealer hedging tends to buy rallies and sell declines, reinforcing direction and increasing the potential for faster movement.

The key idea: GEX is about how the hedge changes, DEX is about the hedge that already exists, and the gamma flip estimates where the market may transition between damping and amplifying behavior.
05 / Beyond price

Volatility and time move the hedge too.

Vanna

Volatility changes delta

Implied volatility can change an option’s delta even when spot is flat. Vanna estimates the hedge adjustment triggered by that volatility move. A sharp post-event volatility drop can therefore produce dealer flow without a new price catalyst.

Charm

Time changes delta

Delta naturally decays as expiration approaches. Charm measures that time-driven drift and the hedging it may require. It helps explain expiration pinning—but only when meaningful OI is concentrated near spot.

Live positioning

Watch what is printing

Sweeps, blocks, UOA, skew, premium flow, and OI walls complement model-derived exposure. They show urgency, concentration, risk pricing, and where positions are building—while still requiring attribution and context.

MODEL_RISK

Public OI has no owner label.

Dealer-side sign conventions are assumptions. Institutional overwriting can flip the assumed side of entire OI clusters.

SURFACE_RISK

Bad volatility inputs compound.

Vanna is especially sensitive to surface fit. Stale quotes and noisy strike-level IV can create misleading exposure.

ATTRIBUTION_RISK

A large print is not always directional.

Sweeps and blocks may be spread legs, collars, hedges, rolls, or portfolio rebalancing rather than outright bets.

REGIME_RISK

The book changes.

Expirations, 0DTE rolling, and new block positioning can move GEX, DEX, and the flip during the session.

06 / Model technicals

Formulas, filters, and sign conventions.

Dealer exposure

GEX, DEX & gamma flip

Calculate exposure across every strike and expiration. The retail convention assumes dealers are long calls and short puts from liquidity provision.

GEX = gamma × OI × multiplier × spot² × 0.01
NET_GEX = Σ(call gamma × call OI) − Σ(put gamma × put OI)
DEX = Σ(call delta × call OI × multiplier) − Σ(put delta × put OI × multiplier)

Gamma flip: recalculate net GEX across hypothetical spot prices in 0.25%–0.5% increments out to ±3%, then interpolate the zero crossing. Recalculate at least once per session and again around material intraday OI changes.

Second-order exposure

Vanna, charm & surface quality

VANNA = −e^(−qT) × d2 × φ(d1) / σ
CHARM = −e^(−qT) × φ(d1) × [2(r−q)T − d2σ√T] / [2Tσ√T]

Aggregate per-contract values using OI and the contract multiplier. For charm, multiply by trading days remaining and compare the cumulative delta shift with normal daily liquidity.

  • Fit each expiration with SVI or a cubic spline.
  • Interpolate term structure in total variance: σ²T.
  • Reject calendar or butterfly arbitrage and validate against realized volatility.
Signal hierarchy

OI distribution and live flow

  • Skew: track rate and direction of change, not only its static level.
  • Net premium: better than raw contract count, but institutional hedges can dominate it.
  • OI walls: strongest when they converge with gamma or flip levels.
  • Max pain: keep low in the hierarchy; it frequently fails.
Data requirements

Use the right chain

GEX, DEX, and flip work require full-chain OI, quotes, and Greeks by strike and expiration. Vanna and charm require a modeled volatility surface. Sweep and block analysis requires print-level tape with venue tagging.

For Nasdaq- or SPY-linked exposure, index and ETF chains are generally deeper and more informative than thinner futures-option chains.

Trust filter

Do not accept the default dealer-side assumption blindly.

Institutional overwriting and concentrated block or sweep activity can reverse the assumed dealer side. Cross-reference exposure against same-strike directional flow; downweight the conflicted portion instead of discarding the entire read.

UOA: volume > 3× its trailing 20-day average and OI < 50% of same-day volumeSWEEP: 3+ venues within 2 seconds at steady or worsening prices from the initial NBBOBLOCK: single print > 500 contracts via negotiated-trade indicator; flow premium filter > $50,000

Level 2: volume, delta, value.

Participation

Volume × range

  • High volume + wide range: efficient initiative movement.
  • High volume + narrow range: a contested level or absorption.
  • Low volume + wide range: one side is absent.
  • Low volume + narrow range: noise.

Use time-of-day volume buckets—not a flat session average—when normalizing participation.

Aggression

Bar delta vs cumulative delta

Bar delta identifies the aggressor now; session cumulative delta identifies control across the day. Divergence across several bars is more useful than one isolated print.

  • Higher price + lower delta = bearish divergence.
  • Lower price + higher delta = bullish divergence.
  • Heavy negative delta with no downside progress can be seller absorption.
Auction structure

VAH, VAL, POC, HVN & LVN

The value area contains 70% of session volume. HVNs show acceptance and act as magnets; LVNs show rejection and often become acceleration corridors. Composite HVNs and the composite POC outrank single-session levels.

VWAP

Mean and extension

Use session VWAP for intraday fair value and anchored VWAP from meaningful structural events for multi-session context. The 1–3 standard-deviation bands measure extension; acceptance beyond a band favors momentum, while confirmed rejection favors mean reversion.

Mechanical VSA thresholds

Stopping volume: volume exceeds 2× the trailing 20-bar average during a down move and the bar closes in its upper third; reverse the close-location rule for buying-side climax/stopping action. Climax: both range and volume exceed 2.5× their trailing 20-bar averages on the same bar. Neither bar is an entry without follow-through.

08 / Footprint

Level 3: see inside the candle.

Imbalance rule

3:1, then stack it

Horizontal and diagonal imbalances require a 3:1 ratio. A stacked zone requires at least three consecutive diagonal imbalances.

STACK_WEIGHT = level_count × average_volume_per_level

A three-level stack at average volume is low priority. A six-plus-level stack with at least 2× average volume per level is high priority.

Passive defense

Absorption

Heavy aggressor volume that fails to move price in its implied direction reveals passive defense. Require a meaningful structural level; high volume with continued price progress is momentum, not absorption.

Forced flow

Trapped traders

Heavy ask volume at a failed high can trap longs; heavy bid volume at a failed low can trap shorts. Their exits can accelerate the reversal after the trap level breaks.

Stored objectives

Unfinished auctions

An extreme with one-sided volume and no useful response marks unfinished business. Store it as a future target—not a guaranteed immediate destination or standalone entry.

Accepted price

Internal bar POC

The price with the most volume inside one footprint bar. Clustering of internal POCs across consecutive bars can reveal a developing HVN and a new acceptance zone.

Live reading order

Ten-second close check

  1. Delta alignment or divergence.
  2. Imbalance formation and location.
  3. Close relative to range and heavy volume.
  4. Bar POC and unfinished extremes.

Delta efficiency confirms exhaustion

Calculate price movement per 100 contracts of delta on every bar of a trend leg. Require declining efficiency for at least four consecutive bars plus a final bar closing against its delta. Validate reversal only when the next bar closes in the opposite direction with volume above its trailing 20-bar average.

DELTA_EFFICIENCY = |price movement| ÷ (|delta| / 100)
09 / Worked setups

Context. Trigger. Risk. Objective.

VSA_01

Value-area counter at the open

CONTEXT

Open above prior VAH.

SIGNAL

First five minutes prints no demand: narrow range, up close, below-average volume, negative delta.

ENTRY / STOP

Short the break of the signal bar’s low; invalidate above its high.

TARGET

Prior VAH, then prior POC.

VSA_02

Stopping-volume reversal

CONTEXT

Price declines into a composite HVN.

SIGNAL

Volume ≥2× average, upper-half close, delta less negative than the prior 2–3 bars.

ENTRY / STOP

Next bar closes above stopping bar midpoint; stop below stopping bar low.

TARGET

Developing VWAP or prior VAL.

VSA_03

Climax-top counter

CONTEXT

Composite resistance is reached.

SIGNAL

Range and volume ≥2.5× average; next bar is narrow, low-volume no demand.

ENTRY / STOP

Third bar closes below climax low; stop above climax high.

TARGET

Prior composite POC.

DELTA_01

Divergence at a structural high

CONTEXT

Price approaches a prior high.

SIGNAL

Delta declines for 4+ consecutive bars and turns negative on the tag.

ENTRY / STOP

Short the tag bar’s low; stop above its high.

TARGET

VWAP, session POC, or prior swing low.

DELTA_02

HVN absorption sequence

CONTEXT

Price approaches a major HVN as cumulative delta declines.

SIGNAL

Negative delta spike cannot break the HVN; next bar turns positive and recovers it.

ENTRY / STOP

Enter on recovery close; stop below the HVN low.

TARGET

Next HVN or VWAP above.

FOOTPRINT_01

Absorption + trap + unfinished auction

CONTEXT

Decline enters composite buy interest with an unfinished low just beneath.

SIGNAL

Three bars of heavy bid volume make less downside progress while ask volume rises as sellers cover.

ENTRY / STOP

Break above the second absorption bar’s high; stop below the absorption-zone low.

TARGET

Nearest stacked ask-imbalance zone or unfinished high.

10 / Execution models

Momentum scalp vs structured reversal.

FAST MODEL / NEGATIVE GAMMA FAVORED

L2 liquidity scalp

Designed to move with momentum into aligned resting orders. Avoid choppy HTF consolidation and treat VWAP plus its bands as no-trade walls.

  • NYAM NQ: 35–80 resting orders inside 5 points.
  • NYAM ES: 100–200 resting orders inside 2 points.
  • Asia ranges: divide by 4. London ranges: divide by 2.5.
  • NQ and ES liquidity must align and fill at roughly the same time.
  • Confirm absorption/LVN acceleration plus clean momentum.
  • Target 15–30 NQ points or 5–10 ES points.
  • Best around 1–1.2R; never exceed 1.5R. Take only 1–2 trades.
REVERSAL MODEL / POSITIVE GAMMA FAVORED

Confluence rejection

Here, VWAP becomes an entry component. Prefer second and third standard-deviation bands, HVN rejection, GEX/DEX levels, gamma flip context, and footprint confirmation.

01 VWAP band + HVN + GEX/DEX
02 VWAP band + GEX/DEX
03 HVN + GEX/DEX
04 VWAP + HVN — lower priority

Place stops at structural invalidation. Go break-even at VWAP/bands, a partial target, or opposing GEX/DEX. Partial into 30–80 NQ or 100–200 ES orders; full target at HTF highs/lows. Typical objective: 3–6R, or 2–4R on prop firms.
